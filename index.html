01

Research

Most autonomous mobile robots rely on LiDAR and depth sensors to see. I'm teaching them to do it with a single 360° camera.

At the Centre for Artificial Intelligence & Robotics, IIT Mandi, I build a 3D map of a robot's surroundings from monocular 360° imagery alone. The map is represented with 3D Gaussian Splatting, and I use it to find obstacles and free space for navigation.

  1. 01 · Capture

    One 360° camera

    A single camera records equirectangular frames: a complete view around the robot at every instant.

  2. 02 · Reconstruct

    Monocular 3DGS map

    Geometry from those frames is optimised into a 3D Gaussian Splatting map: soft, coloured ellipsoids that capture both shape and appearance.

  3. 03 · Perceive

    Obstacles & free space

    The splat map is turned into what the robot can and can't drive through.

  4. 04 · Navigate

    Obstacle-aware motion

    Obstacle information feeds the robot's planning, so it moves safely through the space.

Why camera-only?

  • 1 sensor

    A single camera replaces a multi-sensor rig, reducing cost, weight, power draw and calibration effort.

  • 360°

    Full surround view in every frame, with no blind spots between sensors.

  • 3DGS

    Maps that capture appearance as well as geometry, and can be rendered photo-realistically from any viewpoint.

My degree is in Electronics & Telecommunication with honours in AI/ML, a mix of signals, hardware and deep learning that robotics draws on constantly. At CAIR, IIT Mandi, I work on autonomous mobile robots that perceive their environment through a single 360° camera.
